1. What is this guide and why does it exist?

The Sensitive Qualitative Data Management & Curation Guide (SQDMC Guide) is a free, open-access educational resource designed for graduate students, early-career researchers, and anyone who works with sensitive qualitative data. It provides a structured, step-by-step workflow — from planning and collection through processing, storage, sharing, and long-term archiving or destruction.

This guide began as a course project for the MLIM6338 New Literacies and Technology course at the University of Hong Kong (HKU), completed by a student in the MLIM programme. It also originated from the student's own research journey.

2. How It All Began

When I started writing my thesis, I needed to collect a large volume of textual data related to medical conditions and diseases. The data came from real participants and contained extensive personal and sensitive information. As I worked with this material, two things became increasingly clear.

First, the data had significant research value. If future scholars could reuse it, they could avoid unnecessary duplication of effort. Second, the data also carried serious privacy risks. Direct exposure would harm individuals’ privacy and their trust.

At the same time, I needed to submit an ethics application to the Human Research Ethics Committee (HKU), which required a detailed plan for handling sensitive information. But I had never taken a course on this topic. I knew almost nothing about it.

3. The Struggles of Self-Learning

So I began to learn — and quickly ran into obstacles.

Every time I opened a government or institutional website, I was met with dense policy documents and complex legal texts. I had to dig through them bit by bit, piece together what was relevant, and try to make sense of it all.

Video tutorials existed, but they were scattered with no coherent learning path for beginners. I felt like someone wandering through a maze, taking many wrong turns to gather useful pieces of knowledge.

And most importantly, I wasn't learning this to pass an exam. I needed to actually use this knowledge to handle real data from real participants in my thesis. I wanted a resource that started from the very basics, covered both theory and practice, explained why each step was necessary, and could be immediately applied to real-world situations. But I couldn't find one.

3. Not Just My Struggle

As I talked with friends who were also writing their theses, I noticed some similar questions: How should I store this data? How do I anonymise quotes? Can I share it in the future? So I think, maybe we need a practical, beginner-friendly guide.

Interestingly, data processing itself is a form of data literacy — the ability to collect, produce, and manage data in a responsible and meaningful way. This connects directly to what I was learning in the MLIM6338 New Literacies and Technology.

So I took this course project as an opportunity — to organise everything I had learned, to distill it into a format that I found intuitive, and to share it as an interactive learning guide.

5. Meet Hazel, Your Companion in This Journey

I wanted this guide to be simple, approachable, and practical. So I created Hazel. She is a master's student in Social Sciences conducting qualitative interviews with forest dwellers. She makes mistakes and asks questions, so that others can learn from her missteps, and see the reasoning behind each decision.

Through Hazel's story, I translate abstract concepts into concrete scenarios. I use Hazel's character to share my own real struggles, confusions, and the lessons I learned while handling sensitive data.

6. What I Want to Convey

One message runs through this guide: Anonymisation and research reusability are not binary opposites. The goal is not to choose one over the other, but to find a defensible balance.

We must protect participants' privacy. But we need also preserve what makes qualitative data valuable. This guide is built around this conflict, and it provide a framework for making transparent decisions, so that data can be shared responsibly, without sacrificing either privacy or scholarly value.

7. Interactive Features

This guide is not a static document. It includes interactive features to support active learning:

  • Self-assessments (in Module 1) help you quickly check your own data's sensitivity level.
  • Interactive de-identification demos (in Module 4) show how redaction depth changes across sharing scenarios.
  • Checklists at each lifecycle stage let you track your progress.
  • Case practice (Module 5) lets you work through ethical dilemmas and compare your responses with documented outcomes.

9. Limitations and Disclaimer

This guide is designed for educational reference only. It does not constitute legal, regulatory, or professional advice.
